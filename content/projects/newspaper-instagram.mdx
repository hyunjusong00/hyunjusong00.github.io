## Overview

When I became Editor-in-Chief of Kookmin University Newspaper, the Instagram account mainly functioned as a place to repost finished articles.

Most posts were static images with headlines or short quotes from print stories. Engagement was low, growth had slowed, and most followers were already existing readers from campus.

At the same time, Instagram was increasingly becoming a video-first platform. Reels were dominating both user attention and platform recommendations, especially among students. We realized that continuing to post static article graphics would make it difficult for the newspaper to reach new audiences.

Instead of treating Instagram as just a distribution channel for print content, we decided to build a separate short-form content workflow designed specifically for the platform.

## The problem

The newspaper already had strong written content, but our format no longer matched how students consumed information on social media.

There were several challenges:

- low engagement on static posts
- limited reach outside existing readers
- no established video workflow
- student design team with limited video production experience
- difficulty balancing print deadlines with social media production

The goal was not simply to "make more videos," but to redesign how editorial content could work in a short-form video environment.

## My role

As Editor-in-Chief, I led the overall content and editorial direction for the Instagram account.

My responsibilities included:

- deciding which stories became Reels
- defining narrative direction and tone
- creating production briefs
- coordinating editorial and design teams
- structuring the Reels workflow
- integrating video production into the existing publication process

Rather than directly filming or editing, I focused on building a system that allowed the design team to execute consistently and independently.

## Key decisions

### 1. Treating Reels as editorial content, not promotion

One of the biggest decisions was treating Reels as a real editorial format rather than a marketing add-on.

Every Reel was planned similarly to an article:

- topic selection
- editorial angle
- structure
- intended audience reaction
- publishing schedule

We integrated Reels into the editorial calendar instead of treating them as optional social content.

This helped the team approach video with the same level of seriousness as written journalism.

### 2. Script-first production workflow

Instead of filming large amounts of footage and editing later, every Reel began with a short written script.

The scripts included:

- hook structure
- narrative flow
- approximate timing
- visual direction

This made production more efficient and reduced unnecessary filming time, which was important because the production team consisted of students balancing coursework and publication schedules.

It also forced us to validate whether the content idea itself was strong before spending time producing it.

### 3. Clear editorial handoff system

For each Reel, I prepared a brief containing:

- content angle
- narrative beats
- visual tone references

The goal was to provide enough structure for consistency while still giving the design team creative freedom during execution.

This improved collaboration significantly because the design team no longer needed constant revision cycles or micromanagement.

### 4. Prioritizing consistency over viral spikes

Rather than focusing only on viral content, we focused on maintaining a steady publishing rhythm throughout the semester.

The idea was that repeated exposure to strong Reels would gradually change how students perceived the newspaper account.

Over time, this consistency improved watch time, repeat engagement, and follower growth more effectively than isolated high-performing posts.

## What we built

### Editorial workflow

- Reel briefing system
- Script templates for different content types
- Editorial calendar integration
- Named-editor ownership structure

### Content formats

- campus interviews
- short explainers
- behind-the-scenes coverage
- event coverage
- student-focused short-form storytelling

### Team workflow

- structured editorial-to-design handoff
- reusable production process
- weekly Reel publishing cadence

## Results

- Instagram followers grew approximately **1.5× within one semester**
- Around 200 new followers were gained primarily through Reels content
- Engagement and watch time improved steadily throughout the semester
- The design team expanded from static design work into short-form video production

More importantly, the newspaper developed a repeatable video workflow that future editorial teams could continue building on.

## What I learned

The biggest lesson from this experience was that platforms shape content behavior more than many institutions expect.

Simply reposting existing content into a new platform rarely works well. The format itself changes how stories need to be structured and presented.

I also learned that strong creative direction is often about designing good systems rather than controlling every detail. Clear briefs, narrative structure, and workflow consistency helped the team produce better results than overly detailed supervision would have.

Finally, I learned how quickly creative teams can grow once they develop confidence in a new format. By the end of the semester, the team approached video production much more proactively and experimentally than when we first started.
