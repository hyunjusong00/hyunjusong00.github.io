## Overview

When I became Editor-in-Chief of Kookmin University Newspaper, one issue stood out immediately. The quality of the newspaper's visual and editorial work was not being reflected online.

The website functioned mostly as a traditional article archive. Stories were organized through rigid category menus, article pages looked visually identical, and most of the design work created for each issue disappeared once content moved online.

At the same time, the newspaper already had a strong design team producing high-quality editorial visuals for print editions every week. The problem was not the content itself. It was the digital experience surrounding it.

The redesign project focused on rebuilding the website around actual reader behavior while making editorial design a visible part of the online reading experience.

## The problem

The existing website had several limitations:

- text-heavy article presentation
- outdated navigation structure
- limited visual hierarchy
- weak mobile browsing experience
- minimal integration of editorial design work

Readers had difficulty quickly understanding:

- what stories mattered
- what tone an article had
- what was new in the latest issue

Most articles appeared visually similar until users clicked into them.

This also affected the internal team. Designers spent significant effort creating strong visual identity systems for print issues, but very little of that work was visible on the website itself.

## My role

As Editor-in-Chief, I led the redesign project from planning through launch.

My responsibilities included:

- redesign direction and strategy
- information architecture planning
- editorial UX decisions
- collaboration with the university IT department
- coordination between editorial and design teams
- final review of structure and visual direction

I also worked closely with the school's information administration and IT teams to ensure the redesign could realistically function within the university CMS environment.

## Key decisions

### 1. Treating design as part of the reading experience

One of the biggest decisions was shifting away from a purely text-first article structure.

Instead of using small thumbnails attached to text blocks, we redesigned article presentation so that visual identity became part of how stories were introduced to readers.

This included:

- stronger hero sections
- custom visual treatments
- improved typography
- clearer visual hierarchy
- more intentional use of photography and layout

The goal was to help readers immediately understand the tone and importance of a story before opening it.

### 2. Rebuilding information architecture around reader behavior

The original navigation structure was inherited from older print-era category systems.

However, after reviewing analytics and observing how students browsed the site, it became clear that most readers did not navigate primarily by category.

Instead, readers tended to:

- browse by latest issue
- skim visually before selecting stories
- enter through individual article links

Because of this, the homepage was redesigned around:

- issue-based browsing
- visually driven article discovery
- secondary category filtering rather than category-first navigation

This made the experience feel closer to a digital magazine rather than a static article archive.

### 3. Cross-functional collaboration with university IT

One of the most important parts of the project was collaboration with the university's IT and administration teams.

Since the newspaper website existed inside the university's larger infrastructure, redesign decisions had to consider:

- CMS limitations
- accessibility requirements
- archival compatibility
- technical maintenance constraints

I organized and led multiple working sessions to translate editorial and design goals into technically feasible implementation plans.

This process taught me how important cross-functional communication is in large institutional projects.

### 4. Researching external editorial design references

Before making visual decisions, we researched:

- Korean university newspaper websites
- editorial-focused digital publications
- magazine-style news layouts
- responsive editorial design patterns

The goal was not simply to modernize the site visually, but to rethink how a student newspaper could function online as an editorial product rather than only as an archive.

## What we built

### Website redesign

- issue-focused homepage structure
- redesigned article feed
- improved visual hierarchy
- mobile-first responsive layout
- secondary category navigation

### Editorial design integration

- custom article hero sections
- stronger typography system
- improved image presentation
- visually differentiated story layouts

### Organizational systems

- collaboration process with university IT
- workflow for future website updates
- archive and accessibility compatibility maintained during redesign

## Results

- Website traffic increased by **over 40% within one semester**
- Reader feedback from students and faculty became noticeably more positive
- The design team's work became significantly more visible online
- The newspaper developed a stronger digital identity beyond print publication

Internally, the redesign also improved team morale because the visual work being created each week finally had a stronger public-facing platform.

## What I learned

The biggest lesson from this project was that redesign projects are often more organizational than visual.

Many of the most important decisions involved communication, coordination, and balancing editorial goals with technical limitations rather than purely aesthetic choices.

I also learned that information architecture strongly shapes how people experience content. Small structural decisions, such as prioritizing current issues over rigid category navigation, changed how readers interacted with the entire publication.

Finally, the project reinforced how important presentation surfaces are for creative teams. The design team had already been producing strong work for years. The redesign simply created a better system for that work to actually be seen.
