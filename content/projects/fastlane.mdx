## Overview

Fastlane is an AI-assisted evaluation platform designed to reduce reviewer workload during first-round screening.

The idea started from a simple problem. Organizers receive dozens or sometimes hundreds of submissions, but human judges have limited time and often interpret rubrics differently. Even when everyone tries to evaluate fairly, inconsistency naturally appears as reviewers get tired or focus on different criteria.

Most existing platforms digitize the application process but still leave the actual evaluation work entirely to humans. We wanted to focus on that bottleneck itself.

Instead of trying to fully replace human judgment, Fastlane helps route attention toward the submissions that actually require human review.

## My role

I worked as the PM on a small team and focused on:

- product direction
- evaluation flow design
- rubric-to-agent prompt structure
- organizer and submitter user experience

I mainly worked on defining how the AI evaluation pipeline should behave and how organizers would interact with uncertain or disputed results.

## Key product decisions

### 1. Three-pass evaluation system

Rather than relying on a single LLM response, we designed a three-step evaluation structure.

- **Draft**: evaluates the strongest interpretation of the submission
- **Skeptic**: identifies weaknesses, risks, and unclear areas
- **Judge**: compares both perspectives and produces the final score using the rubric as reference

This structure helped us detect disagreement inside the AI process itself. If the outputs were highly inconsistent, the submission would automatically be escalated for human review.

### 2. Variance-based escalation

Most evaluation systems escalate submissions based on low scores. We approached it differently.

Instead of asking "Which submissions scored badly?", we asked: "Which submissions is the AI least confident about?"

Submissions with high disagreement between evaluation passes were prioritized for human review, while high-confidence cases could move forward automatically.

This shifted the product away from "AI replaces judges" toward "AI helps humans spend time where judgment matters most."

### 3. Simplified submission experience

We also focused heavily on the applicant experience.

Many submission platforms feel overly administrative and difficult to use, especially for students or early-stage founders. We simplified the flow by supporting:

- PDF or link uploads
- short structured responses
- lightweight validation
- faster completion flow

The goal was to reduce friction while still collecting structured information for evaluation.

## What we built

- Live MVP deployment
- Rubric-driven prompt generation
- Multi-pass AI scoring pipeline
- Variance tracking and escalation queue
- Real-time competition leaderboard
- Audit trail for evaluation decisions

## What I learned

The biggest lesson from Fastlane was that AI evaluation systems work best when they support human judgment rather than replace it.

The most important product question was not "Can AI evaluate correctly?" It was "Can AI recognize when a human should step in?"

That idea changed the way I think about AI products more broadly. Good AI systems are often less about removing humans from decisions and more about helping people focus their attention where it matters most.
